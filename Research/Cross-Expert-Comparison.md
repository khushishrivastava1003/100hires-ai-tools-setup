# Cross-Expert Comparison

**Project:** AI-Powered SEO Content Production Playbook  
**Source base:** `Research/Expert-Shortlists/`, `Research/Master-Recommendations.md`, and `Research/Recommendation-Matrix.md` only.  
**Purpose:** Pattern analysis across completed research. This is not the final playbook.

## Business Goals

- **Aimee Jurenka, Freddie Chatt, and Matt Kenyon** agree that content should start with business meaning, purpose, or offer clarity—not tools or volume.
- Aimee frames this as alignment across brand meaning, topics, content, and third-party signals (MR-001).
- Freddie frames it as assigning every content asset a job such as earning links, driving sales, building awareness, or growing the email list (MR-002).
- Matt frames it as returning to first principles before choosing SEO or AI tactics (MR-003).

### Different angles

- **Aimee** emphasizes brand meaning and external signal consistency.
- **Freddie** emphasizes commercial accountability for each asset.
- **Matt** emphasizes strategic simplification around the core offer.

### Disagreements

No direct disagreement. The differences reflect emphasis rather than conflicting recommendations.

### Combinations

- Aimee defines what the brand should be known for (MR-001).
- Freddie turns that positioning into measurable content jobs (MR-002).
- Matt filters out tactics that do not support the offer (MR-003).

## Strongest Areas of Consensus

1. **AI search visibility requires structured measurement.** Eight experts are associated with similar ideas in the master recommendations: Aimee, Aleyda, Freddie, Ivan, Matt, Nathan, Nicolas, and Steve. This is strong consensus because the recommendations repeatedly reject casual AI checks and instead emphasize prompt libraries, repeated samples, citations, visibility metrics, product-level measurement, and business impact.

2. **Keyword and topic research must expand beyond head terms.** Eight experts support broader keyword/topic research through prompts, tasks, constraints, long-tail ecommerce keywords, product attributes, GSC regex, query fan-out, intent analysis, and buyer deal-breakers.

3. **Content decisions should start from business goals, audience journeys, or buyer needs.** Seven experts support this pattern. The research repeatedly links content choices to brand meaning, buyer pain points, commercial jobs, product attributes, deal-breakers, or business impact.

4. **Existing content should be audited, refreshed, restructured, or validated before producing more.** Seven experts support some version of this idea. The evidence appears in cannibalization checks, structure improvements, ecommerce audits, implementation QA, SEO foundations, and content-refresh loops.

5. **Human judgment and QA remain necessary in AI workflows.** Six experts support this pattern. The research repeatedly frames AI as useful for acceleration, research, drafting, automation, or planning, but not as a fully autonomous strategic or publishing authority.

6. **Owned content and third-party sources both shape AI search answers.** Seven experts support this pattern. The research covers brand ecosystems, third-party citation surfaces, comparison content, owned deal-breaker pages, product-level visibility, and source ecosystem mapping.

## Major Expert Disagreements

The research contains few clean contradictions. Most differences are emphasis, market context, or workflow layer. These are the strongest tensions for a later “Where Experts Differ” section.

### 1. AI search optimization first vs SEO foundations first

- **Topic:** Strategy
- **Experts involved:** Aleyda Solís, Nicolas Gorroño, Freddie Chatt, Kai Cromwell
- **Aleyda’s viewpoint:** Start AI search optimization by diagnosing prompts, journeys, source ecosystems, gaps, visibility, and business impact before changing content (MR-047).
- **Nicolas’s viewpoint:** Fix SEO foundations first: indexed pages, fast performance, clear service pages, schema, useful content, entity consistency, and aligned profiles before AI-specific tactics (MR-050).
- **Freddie’s viewpoint:** Ecommerce teams should audit foundational SEO issues before producing or scaling more content (MR-048).
- **Kai’s viewpoint:** Ecommerce audits should prioritize revenue-moving actions rather than generic tool exports (MR-049).
- **Evidence supporting each viewpoint:** Aleyda’s evidence is a structured AI search diagnostic framework (MR-047). Nicolas and Freddie rely on foundation-first SEO readiness checks (MR-048, MR-050). Kai adds revenue prioritization to the audit layer (MR-049).
- **Why this is a tension:** These experts agree on diagnosis before execution, but they prioritize different diagnostic starting points: AI search presence, technical/content foundations, ecommerce foundations, or revenue-moving opportunities.

### 2. Automation scope: automate aggressively vs keep AI auxiliary and supervised

- **Topic:** Scaling / Team Workflow
- **Experts involved:** Freddie Chatt, Ivan Palii, Nathan Gotch, Steve Toth
- **Freddie’s viewpoint:** Automate repetitive SEO data collection with AI and APIs so humans can focus on strategy (MR-041).
- **Ivan’s viewpoint:** Use Claude as an auxiliary automation layer connected to SEO data sources, not as a full replacement for SEO tools (MR-042).
- **Nathan’s viewpoint:** Keep AI agents scoped, tested, and supervised by experienced SEO judgment (MR-046).
- **Steve’s viewpoint:** Turn AI sessions into reusable SOPs, but preserve process knowledge and refinements (MR-019).
- **Evidence supporting each viewpoint:** Freddie’s recommendation supports automation of reporting, keyword seed lists, competitor monitoring, rankings, backlinks, on-page audits, AI Overview presence, and SERP composition (MR-041). Ivan explicitly limits Claude to an auxiliary role (MR-042). Nathan warns against letting agents loose on high-stakes SEO (MR-046). Steve turns AI work into SOP assets rather than one-off automation (MR-019).
- **Why this is a tension:** They agree AI can scale work, but differ in how much operational responsibility AI should carry.

### 3. Content production vs content restraint

- **Topic:** Content Brief / Content Refreshing
- **Experts involved:** Matt Kenyon, Nathan Gotch, Aimee Jurenka, Freddie Chatt
- **Matt’s production viewpoint:** Build one keyword into one content piece through a structured intent-to-publish workflow (MR-014).
- **Nathan’s production viewpoint:** Treat AI-assisted SEO content as an end-to-end production system from keyword selection to post-publish optimization (MR-015).
- **Aimee’s restraint viewpoint:** Restructure existing content and strengthen meaning/internal structure before defaulting to more content (MR-028).
- **Freddie’s restraint viewpoint:** Audit ecommerce SEO foundations before producing more content (MR-048).
- **Evidence supporting each viewpoint:** Matt and Nathan provide production workflows (MR-014, MR-015). Aimee and Freddie provide pre-production or refresh-first checks (MR-028, MR-048).
- **Why this is a tension:** They do not contradict each other, but they imply different default actions depending on site condition: build new assets when research justifies it, or pause production when structure, cannibalization, or foundations are weak.

### 4. AI search measurement level: prompt-level, product-level, source-level, or business-impact level

- **Topic:** AI Search Visibility / Performance Measurement
- **Experts involved:** Aimee Jurenka, Aleyda Solís, Freddie Chatt, Ivan Palii, Nathan Gotch, Steve Toth
- **Aimee’s viewpoint:** Use repeated prompt samples rather than screenshots (MR-030).
- **Aleyda’s viewpoint:** Use representative prompt libraries and separate presence, citations, traffic, page type, and business impact (MR-031, MR-037).
- **Freddie’s viewpoint:** Track product-level AI-shopping visibility such as SKU appearance, position, quoted price, recommendation wins, and competitors (MR-032).
- **Ivan’s viewpoint:** Track mentions, sentiment, competitors, citation sources, and multiple methods beyond one metric (MR-033, MR-038).
- **Nathan’s viewpoint:** Track AI coverage, position, share of voice, and citations (MR-035).
- **Steve’s viewpoint:** Track the full third-party citation surface shaping brand representation (MR-036).
- **Evidence supporting each viewpoint:** Each expert contributes a different measurement object: prompt samples, prompt libraries, product visibility, mention/sentiment/citation metrics, coverage/share of voice, or citation surfaces.
- **Why this is a tension:** They agree measurement matters, but emphasize different units of analysis.

## Research Insights

1. **AI Search Visibility is the strongest evidence cluster.** It has the highest number of primary recommendations in the matrix and appears repeatedly in the consensus section.

2. **The research is more about workflow design than AI drafting.** The matrix contains no primary-stage AI Drafting recommendation, while many recommendations focus on briefing, prompting, QA, measurement, refreshing, and scaling.

3. **Most experts treat AI as a leverage layer, not a replacement for SEO judgment.** Nathan, Ivan, Nicolas, Steve, Matt, and Mark all introduce some form of supervision, validation, planning, QA, or auxiliary use.

4. **Measurement discipline is a major theme.** Aimee, Aleyda, Ivan, Nathan, Freddie, Steve, and Nicolas all warn against simplistic AI visibility interpretation in different ways.

5. **Ecommerce is well represented but not universal.** Freddie and Kai provide strong ecommerce-specific recommendations, while Steve is more B2B buyer-deal-breaker focused and Aleyda offers broader AI search measurement frameworks.

6. **Content quality is framed as specificity, evidence, and brand differentiation.** Aimee, Mark, Nathan, Nicolas, and Steve all support moving away from generic AI output.

7. **Several stages are under-covered as primary evidence.** The matrix shows no primary recommendations for SERP Analysis, AI Drafting, EEAT, or Internal Linking, even though some appear inside broader workflows.

8. **The strongest operational pattern is diagnosis before production.** Experts repeatedly recommend checking business goals, SEO foundations, content structure, measurement data, or AI search visibility before scaling output.

9. **Source ecosystems matter alongside owned content.** Aimee, Aleyda, Ivan, Matt, Nathan, Freddie, and Steve all point toward the role of third-party signals, citations, mentions, comparison sources, or external validation.

10. **Some recommendations are deliberately cautious or niche.** Raw HTML accessibility, synthetic AI-tracker prompt detection, AEO comparison package evaluation, and product-level AI-shopping tracking are valuable but more situational than the broad consensus recommendations.
