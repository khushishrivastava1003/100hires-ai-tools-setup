### 3. Separate AI presence, citations, traffic, page type, and business impact

- **Confidence:** High

### 5. Make important content available in raw HTML for AI-assistant grounding

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Do AI Assistants Actually Render Your JavaScript when Grounding?”
- **Date:** 18 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_do-ai-assistants-actually-render-your-activity-7473307747128733697-YIWg](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_do-ai-assistants-actually-render-your-activity-7473307747128733697-YIWg)
- **Recommendation:** Do not assume AI assistants render JavaScript when grounding answers. If important content is injected client-side, lazy-loaded, hidden behind hydrated tabs, or absent from raw HTML, it may effectively be invisible to major AI assistants. Use server logs rather than chatbot self-reports to verify what AI systems actually fetched.
- **Evidence:** “Trust the server log, never the chatbot’s description of itself.” / “If your important content is injected by JavaScript… that content effectively does not exist.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a technical publishing requirement for AI-search content. It ensures that content created by the playbook is actually accessible to crawlers, assistants, and grounding systems.
- **Stage:** Technical SEO, publishing, crawl/accessibility checks, AI-search readiness, content QA
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** Aleyda is summarizing Andre Alpar’s test, not presenting her own experiment. The exact behavior may change by platform and over time, so teams should verify with logs and current tests.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly event/newsletter/community posts, short platform-news reactions, or external-study summaries that overlapped with stronger prompt-library, checklist, measurement, and publishing-readiness sources. The existing report was used only for cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Aleyda Solís’s overall viewpoint is that AI search should be optimized and measured through structured diagnosis, not hype or isolated visibility screenshots. She repeatedly separates prompts, journeys, source ecosystems, citations, traffic, readiness, and business impact so teams can know what they are actually improving. For content production, her contribution is strongest at the research, measurement, and validation stages: define representative prompts, understand AI-assisted journeys, identify which owned and third-party sources shape answers, and report results without overclaiming. She also keeps the work grounded in technical accessibility, product/local/feed readiness, and traditional SEO fundamentals.

## Strongest Recommendation

Build a representative AI-search prompt library tied to real business journeys, then use it to diagnose where the brand is visible, cited, recommended, misrepresented, or missing before producing or refreshing content.

## Weakest / Least Supported

The JavaScript/rendering warning is important but least stable because assistant crawling and rendering behavior changes quickly and the post summarizes someone else’s experiment. It should be treated as a testing requirement rather than a permanent universal rule.
