## Selected Sources

### 1. Target long-tail commercial ecommerce keywords first

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “The #1 mistake ecommerce sites make with SEO:”
- **Date:** 29 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_the-1-mistake-ecommerce-sites-make-with-activity-7466016740032819200-1Txv](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_the-1-mistake-ecommerce-sites-make-with-activity-7466016740032819200-1Txv)
- **Recommendation:** Ecommerce brands should avoid starting with overly broad, highly competitive head terms. Instead, identify the product’s USP and target long-tail commercial queries that are easier to rank for and more likely to convert.
- **Evidence:** “Focus on the USP of your jewellery.” / “Targeting longer tail keywords will get you much quicker results.” / “Those terms are much less competitive. And also much more likely to convert.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a clear keyword-research SOP rule for ecommerce content production. It helps teams brief product/category/supporting content around realistic, conversion-oriented opportunities rather than vanity keywords.
- **Stage:** Keyword research, audience and business goals, search intent, ecommerce content briefs
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The example is illustrative, not a measured case. Keyword difficulty and conversion likelihood still need validation with SERP, product-margin, and demand data.

### 2. Audit ecommerce SEO before producing more content

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “10 signs that your ecommerce website may have SEO problems”
- **Date:** 30 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_10-signs-that-your-ecommerce-website-may-activity-7466507467309428736-3Q3F](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_10-signs-that-your-ecommerce-website-may-activity-7466507467309428736-3Q3F)
- **Recommendation:** Before scaling content, ecommerce teams should audit foundational problems: lack of customer research, Shopify product URL structure, stale blog content, broken high-authority links, weak homepage titles, 404ing old products, unoptimized collection pages, duplicate or generic product descriptions, and lack of technical SEO tooling.
- **Evidence:** “You’ve never spoken to your customers to understand their pain points.” / “You’ve never optimised your collection pages.” / “You don’t have unique product descriptions.”
- **Why it matters:** This gives an ecommerce-specific pre-production checklist. It prevents AI content work from covering up basic technical, product-page, and customer-understanding gaps.
- **Stage:** SEO audit, audience research, technical SEO, product/category pages, content quality
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** It is a checklist, not a prioritized diagnostic model. Teams should prioritize items by revenue impact, indexation/crawl impact, and effort.

### 3. Assign every content asset a commercial job

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Most content is forgotten the second it’s published.”
- **Date:** 4 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_most-content-is-forgotten-the-second-its-activity-7468194950720024578-rddU](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_most-content-is-forgotten-the-second-its-activity-7468194950720024578-rddU)
- **Recommendation:** Brands should stop publishing content just to publish. Every content asset should have a defined job: earn links, drive sales, build awareness, or grow the email list.
- **Evidence:** “The brands winning right now aren’t pumping out more content.” / “They’re publishing content with a job to do.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a strong production-governance rule. It helps decide what to brief, what to reject, and how to measure success after publishing.
- **Stage:** Content strategy, content briefs, publishing governance, measuring content performance
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** The post is partly a resource announcement and does not provide examples or measurement criteria for each content job.

### 4. Automate SEO data collection so humans can focus on strategy

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “I’ve spent the last 6 months trying to automate myself out of my own business.”
- **Date:** 5 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_ive-spent-the-last-6-months-trying-to-automate-activity-7468557424497553408-9fFf](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_ive-spent-the-last-6-months-trying-to-automate-activity-7468557424497553408-9fFf)
- **Recommendation:** Use AI and data APIs to automate repetitive SEO tasks such as client reporting, keyword research seed lists, competitor monitoring, AI-search visibility tracking, rankings, backlinks, on-page audits, AI Overview presence, competitor benchmarks, and SERP composition. Keep human time for judgment and strategy.
- **Evidence:** “The data pulling was never my value.” / “Client reporting: automated. Keyword research seed lists: automated. Competitor monitoring: automated. AI search visibility tracking: automated.” / “A full client audit that used to eat a day now takes 20 minutes.”
- **Why it matters:** This directly supports AI-powered production operations. It shows what parts of SEO content production can be automated without pretending that strategy, prioritization, and client judgment should disappear.
- **Stage:** AI workflow automation, keyword research, competitor analysis, reporting, AI-search visibility measurement, scaling production
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The post promotes DataForSEO via Freddie’s link and contains self-reported time savings. Treat the workflow categories as strong; verify exact tool economics and savings independently.

### 5. Measure product-level AI-shopping visibility, not only brand mentions

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “One of my products showed up in every single ChatGPT shopping answer I tracked last week.”
- **Date:** 17 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_one-of-my-products-showed-up-in-every-single-activity-7472937957172211712-wU6](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_one-of-my-products-showed-up-in-every-single-activity-7472937957172211712-wU6)_
- **Recommendation:** Ecommerce brands should measure how individual SKUs appear in AI-shopping answers: visibility, position, quoted price, recommendation wins, and competitors taking the recommendation. Brand-level mention tracking is not enough.
- **Evidence:** “Visibility and winning are two completely different things.” / “The real one is which product gets recommended, at what price, against who.”
- **Why it matters:** This adds an ecommerce-specific AI-search measurement layer. It changes the content-production question from “is the brand mentioned?” to “which products win recommendations, and what content/data might influence that?”
- **Stage:** AI-search visibility measurement, ecommerce product content, competitor analysis, product-page optimization, conversion-focused reporting
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The example uses Peec AI’s shopping tool and Freddie’s own product data. The framework is valuable, but the tool’s methodology and representativeness should be verified before generalizing.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly personal/promotional posts, ecommerce tips with less SOP detail, repeated AI-search ideas, or examples better covered by selected keyword, audit, content-job, automation, and product-visibility sources. Existing reports/compiled material were used only for cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Freddie Chatt’s overall viewpoint is that ecommerce SEO should stay commercially grounded: prioritize the pages, products, and keywords most likely to create revenue. He treats AI as an operational accelerator for data collection, reporting, competitor monitoring, and AI-search visibility tracking, not as a replacement for strategic judgment. His content is especially useful for ecommerce-specific playbook stages: long-tail commercial keyword selection, product/category page optimization, content purpose, and product-level AI-shopping measurement. He repeatedly warns against doing SEO as vague activity rather than tying it to conversion, sales, and customer understanding.

## Strongest Recommendation

For ecommerce, start with commercially realistic long-tail opportunities and product/category pages, then use automation to speed up data gathering while humans focus on strategy.

## Weakest / Least Supported

The exact claim that a full client audit can go from a day to 20 minutes with Claude and DataForSEO is self-reported and tool-specific. It is useful as a workflow direction, but the time saving should be verified in each team’s environment.
