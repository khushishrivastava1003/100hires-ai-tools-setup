## Selected Sources

### 1. Do not measure AI-search visibility with only one metric

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “The worst thing you can do in your AEO strategy is to choose only one metric for tracking AI search visibility.”
- **Date:** 12 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_how-to-monitor-ai-search-visibility-properly-activity-7471197539103301632-cTh6](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_how-to-monitor-ai-search-visibility-properly-activity-7471197539103301632-cTh6)
- **Recommendation:** AI search visibility should be monitored with multiple methods because AI chats are only one step in a longer journey across Google, YouTube, Reddit, and other platforms. A single North Star metric or revenue-only channel view is too narrow for understanding AI-search impact.
- **Evidence:** “AI chats became one of multiple steps in a long user journey, alongside Google, YouTube, Reddit, and so on.”
- **Why it matters:** This gives the playbook a measurement philosophy. AI-powered content production should not be judged only by rankings, citations, or direct AI referral traffic; it needs a broader visibility and attribution view.
- **Stage:** Measuring AI-search visibility, measuring SEO performance, attribution, reporting
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** The post links to a more detailed guide but does not include all five methods in the scraped text. Use this source as evidence of the principle, not the complete framework.

### 2. Track brand mentions, sentiment, competitors, and citation sources — not only page impressions

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “GSC will never build an AI performance tracking report the way it should be.”
- **Date:** 8 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_gsc-will-never-build-an-ai-performance-tracking-activity-7469719044682797056-OLA7](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_gsc-will-never-build-an-ai-performance-tracking-activity-7469719044682797056-OLA7)
- **Recommendation:** Google Search Console and GA4 are essential, but they are not enough for complete AI visibility analytics. Teams also need brand mention tracking, brand sentiment tracking, competitor visibility comparison, source/entity tracking, rankings, site health, and AI visibility data in one reporting system.
- **Evidence:** “We need brand mentions and brand sentiment tracking.” / “We want to compare our visibility with competitors’ visibility.”
- **Why it matters:** It defines what a mature AI-content reporting layer should include after publishing. This helps teams evaluate whether content is shaping AI recommendations, not just whether a page received impressions.
- **Stage:** AI-search visibility measurement, competitor analysis, reporting dashboards, post-publish analysis
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Ivan works at Sitechecker and positions Sitechecker as the solution, so there is a clear commercial interest. The measurement requirements are still useful, but tool recommendations should be treated separately from the framework.

### 3. Use Claude as an auxiliary automation layer, not a full replacement for SEO tools

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “This is the first study on how SEO agencies use Claude for automation.”
- **Date:** 25 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_how-agencies-use-claude-for-seo-automations-activity-7464596131444846594-jBLV](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_how-agencies-use-claude-for-seo-automations-activity-7464596131444846594-jBLV)
- **Recommendation:** Agencies should treat Claude as an emerging automation layer connected to core SEO data sources such as GSC and GA4, while recognizing unresolved problems: team training, hallucinations, context limits, API cost unpredictability, and tasks better handled in native tools such as dashboards, large tables, alerts, and client report sharing.
- **Evidence:** “Only 10% said that Claude has radically reduced their interaction with SEO tools.” / “Tasks that are not convenient to solve in Claude… graphs and dashboards… large tables… alerts… sharing reports.”
- **Why it matters:** This adds operational realism to AI-powered content production. AI can support research, reporting, and automation, but production systems still need specialized tools, QA, and team training.
- **Stage:** AI workflow design, team training, reporting, quality control, scaling content production
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Survey sample is 30 agencies, so it is useful directional evidence but not a definitive industry benchmark.

### 4. Detect synthetic AI-tracker prompts before interpreting GSC data

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “A pattern that says that such a long query is from an AI prompt tracker, not from a real user.”
- **Date:** 7 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_a-pattern-that-says-that-such-a-long-query-activity-7469322115637243904-nIKd](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_a-pattern-that-says-that-such-a-long-query-activity-7469322115637243904-nIKd)
- **Recommendation:** When analyzing GSC query data, identify synthetic queries created by AI visibility trackers. Very long queries containing multiple “or” rules and ICP parameters may represent bot/tracker prompts rather than real user behavior.
- **Evidence:** “Nobody uses search and AI chats this way.” / “If the query contains multiple ‘or’ rules, it’s almost 100% a bot.”
- **Why it matters:** It protects the measurement stage from bad interpretation. If AI content teams mistake tracker-generated prompts for real demand, they may brief, optimize, or report against artificial signals.
- **Stage:** Keyword/topic research validation, GSC analysis, AI-search measurement, reporting QA
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** The “almost 100%” language is strong and based on pattern observation. It should be used as a diagnostic warning, not an absolute rule.

### 5. Evaluate AEO comparison packages by ownership, durability, and source trust

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “A brand asked if G2’s $3,700 AEO comparison package is worth it.”
- **Date:** 19 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_a-brand-asked-if-g2s-3700-aeo-comparison-activity-7473632825259323392-occj](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ivanpalii_a-brand-asked-if-g2s-3700-aeo-comparison-activity-7473632825259323392-occj)
- **Recommendation:** Before paying for comparison/AEO content, evaluate whether the brand can keep the asset, reuse the data, update it, and whether AI systems will treat it as more trustworthy than competitor-created or third-party comparison tables. Do not buy comparison content only because “AI chats love tables, comparisons, and numbers.”
- **Evidence:** “For many big brands, the goal is not to be visible… the goal is to be recommended as the best option.” / “If you have to pay for it every quarter or year to keep the content on the site, it is definitely a bad deal.”
- **Why it matters:** It gives a procurement and content-quality filter for AI-search assets. Comparison content can support AI visibility, but teams should assess ownership, updateability, trust, and strategic value before investing.
- **Stage:** Competitor/SERP analysis, comparison content, AI-search visibility, content quality, budget prioritization
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** Ivan recommends spending on “regular link insertions” instead, which may carry search-policy risk depending on whether links are paid and how they are qualified. Treat that part cautiously.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly promotional/tool posts, narrower reporting examples, repeated AI-visibility ideas, or posts outside content-production scope. The existing report was used only for background and cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Ivan Palii’s viewpoint is that AI SEO needs serious measurement discipline. He is less focused on how to draft content and more focused on how agencies should evaluate whether AI-search visibility, citations, recommendations, rankings, and business outcomes are actually improving. His recurring argument is that GSC and GA4 remain foundational, but AI visibility requires additional layers: brand mentions, sentiment, competitor comparison, prompt/source tracking, and careful filtering of synthetic data. He also treats AI tools such as Claude as useful workflow accelerators, but not replacements for native SEO dashboards, large-table analysis, alerts, or client reporting systems.

## Strongest Recommendation

Do not rely on one metric for AI-search visibility. Combine GSC/GA4, rankings, brand mentions, sentiment, competitor visibility, source/citation tracking, and attribution signals to understand whether AI-powered content is influencing discovery and recommendations.

## Weakest / Least Supported

The recommendation to spend money on link insertions instead of a paid comparison/AEO package needs caution. It may be commercially sensible in Ivan’s example, but link insertions can create search-policy risk depending on whether they are paid, disclosed, and qualified.
