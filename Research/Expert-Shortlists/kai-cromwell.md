## Selected Sources

### 1. Use Claude-assisted research to build high-ticket buying guides

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Do this if you sell a $500+ product.”
- **Date:** 12 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_do-this-if-you-sell-a-500-product-1-activity-7471056302073569280-c2v-](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_do-this-if-you-sell-a-500-product-1-activity-7471056302073569280-c2v-)
- **Recommendation:** For high-ticket ecommerce products, use Claude to collect buyer pain points from Reddit, Amazon reviews, YouTube comments, and low-star competitor reviews. Then write a buying guide covering costs, delivery, installation, maintenance, objections, and other pre-purchase concerns. Add internal links from supporting content to the guide and from the guide to money pages.
- **Evidence:** “Use Claude to scrape Reddit to find all of the pain points.” / “Write a buying guide that explains every single thing a person needs to know before they buy this $500 thing online.” / “Build internal links from it to your target money pages.”
- **Why it matters:** This is Kai’s clearest AI-powered content-production workflow. It combines audience research, AI-assisted synthesis, buyer education, internal linking, and commercial page support.
- **Stage:** Audience research, AI-assisted research, content briefs, AI-assisted drafting, internal linking, ecommerce content strategy
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The post says a brand made `$8.5k` from buying guides, but does not provide analytics exports, attribution method, timeframe, or conversion/profit data.

### 2. Expand ecommerce category pages by product attributes

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “99% of SEO campaigns leave money on the table.”
- **Date:** 5 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_99-of-seo-campaigns-leave-money-on-the-table-activity-7468519591481319424-mx5l](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_99-of-seo-campaigns-leave-money-on-the-table-activity-7468519591481319424-mx5l)
- **Recommendation:** Classify a product line in as many commercially meaningful ways as possible, then build and rank a page for each attribute/category variation rather than only targeting the broad head term.
- **Evidence:** “Classify your product line as many ways as possible, then build and rank a page for each way.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a practical topic/keyword expansion method for ecommerce. It helps convert product attributes into content/page briefs for long-tail category demand.
- **Stage:** Keyword research, topic research, category-page strategy, search intent, ecommerce site architecture
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Search volume examples are given, but teams still need to validate demand, indexation risk, duplication risk, and whether each attribute deserves a separate indexable page.

### 3. Prioritize collection/category-page optimization for large catalogs

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “There’s only one thing to do if you… have a large product catalog…”
- **Date:** 2 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_theres-only-one-thing-to-do-if-you-1-activity-7467665942974746624-9Q8a](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_theres-only-one-thing-to-do-if-you-1-activity-7467665942974746624-9Q8a)
- **Recommendation:** For large ecommerce catalogs that are not earning SEO revenue, optimize category pages first. Add short copy above the product grid, more detailed supporting copy below the grid, and internal links to similar collections.
- **Evidence:** “Just optimize your category pages.” / “Add ~50 words above the product grid, then 250-300 below the grid. Add internal links to similar collections.”
- **Why it matters:** It gives a concrete on-page ecommerce production pattern for collection pages, which can be turned into a repeatable brief/template.
- **Stage:** On-page SEO, category-page content, internal linking, content refreshing, ecommerce publishing
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** The post reports organic search revenue up 44% after 30 collection optimizations, but does not disclose analytics, controls, seasonality, or other concurrent changes. The “90% of your SEO strategy” claim is too broad and should be treated as a heuristic for large-catalog ecommerce, not a universal rule.

### 4. Build a boring monthly ecommerce SEO cadence

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “The most boring SEO campaign in the world made $369k in the last 12 months.”
- **Date:** 5 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_the-most-boring-seo-campaign-in-the-world-activity-7468791102972694528-GX6a](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_the-most-boring-seo-campaign-in-the-world-activity-7468791102972694528-GX6a)
- **Recommendation:** For large ecommerce sites, run a consistent monthly cadence: optimize a fixed number of collection pages, build a small number of backlinks, and publish best-product-for-use-case roundup articles.
- **Evidence:** “This is what we do for them every month: optimize 15 collections; build 6 backlinks; 2-3x best {{product}} for {{use case}} roundup articles.”
- **Why it matters:** This source provides a scalable production rhythm: commercial page optimization plus supporting roundup content plus authority building. It is useful for turning the playbook into monthly operations.
- **Stage:** Scaling content production, ecommerce SEO operations, content calendar, collection-page optimization, product roundups, measurement
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Revenue and ROI are self-reported. “Build 6 backlinks” needs quality and policy qualification; paid or manipulative links can create search-policy risk.

### 5. Audit ecommerce SEO by revenue-moving actions, not tool exports

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “You paid $2,000 for an SEO audit. What you got was fleeced.”
- **Date:** 12 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_you-paid-2000-for-an-seo-audit-what-you-activity-7470994874130075648-i2I](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_you-paid-2000-for-an-seo-audit-what-you-activity-7470994874130075648-i2I)_
- **Recommendation:** Ecommerce audits should identify the highest-impact ranking/revenue opportunities: target keywords for every product and category page, optimization of those pages, internal-link structure, backlink activity, and recent bottom-/middle-funnel supporting content. Avoid getting paralyzed by generic tool-export issue lists.
- **Evidence:** “Does your site have a clear target keyword for every product and category page?” / “Have you written any supporting BoF and/or MoF blog content in the last 3 months?” / “It should identify the key wins that will actually change your rankings.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a strong quality gate before content production. It tells teams what to inspect before creating more AI-assisted content.
- **Stage:** SEO audit, content prioritization, product/category pages, internal linking, BoF/MoF content, publishing governance
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** Kai downplays technical SEO, schema, canonicals, and page speed too broadly. Those items may be foundational depending on site condition, crawlability, rendering, and ecommerce complexity.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly AEO-skepticism memes, personal posts, short revenue rebuttals, repeated ecommerce ideas, or narrower examples already covered by the selected category, buying-guide, cadence, and audit sources. Existing reports/compiled material were used only for cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Kai Cromwell’s overall viewpoint is that ecommerce SEO should be judged by revenue, not hype, rankings, screenshots, or generic audits. He emphasizes category and collection pages, long-tail commercial category expansion, high-ticket buying guides, product roundups, competitor content, internal links, and authority building. His AI usage is practical and constrained: use Claude to speed up buyer research from reviews, Reddit, YouTube comments, and competitor feedback, then rely on human strategy and editing to turn that into useful commercial content. He is skeptical of AEO/GEO work that cannot show revenue impact.

## Strongest Recommendation

For high-ticket ecommerce products, use AI-assisted customer-pain research to create buying guides that answer real purchase objections and internally link to money pages.

## Weakest / Least Supported

His broad dismissal of technical SEO as low-impact is the least-supported recommendation. Technical SEO may be less commercially important than category/content work on some healthy ecommerce sites, but crawlability, indexation, canonicals, structured data, rendering, and speed can be decisive on others.
