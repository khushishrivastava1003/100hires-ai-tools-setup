## Selected Sources

### 1. Use People Also Ask / intent-proximity questions to find content gaps

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: Google uses TTR (Time To Result) as an internal success metric…”
- **Date:** 17 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7472946532959457281-1Hbz](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7472946532959457281-1Hbz)
- **Recommendation:** Use “time to result” thinking and People Also Ask-style intent-proximity questions to identify what users are likely to ask next. Compare those questions against an existing page to find which related needs are fully answered, partially answered, or missing.
- **Evidence:** “What a user is likely to ask next.” / “Do an analysis of which of these questions you fully answer, partially answer, or don't answer at all.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a highly practical way to turn audience intent into a content brief or refresh brief. It helps teams move beyond one keyword and build pages that solve the full search journey.
- **Stage:** Search intent, competitor/SERP research, content briefs, content refreshing, content quality
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The post references independent correlation studies but does not include the underlying data in the scraped material. The method is still operationally strong.

### 2. Mine Google Search Console queries with regex before relying on vague AI classification

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: Instead of spicy 'probably ok' AI, you can reliably* filter your GSC query data…”
- **Date:** 11 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7470766265666580480-FvFN](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7470766265666580480-FvFN)
- **Recommendation:** Use regex filters in Google Search Console to segment real query data into informational, comparison, product/service, transactional, navigational, SaaS/tool, and long-question patterns. Use those query groups to uncover content opportunities and diagnose what your site is already ranking for.
- **Evidence:** “Open your Google Search Console.” / “Select ‘Query’.” / “Change the drop down to ‘Custom (regex)’.” / “Paste in the regex.”
- **Why it matters:** This gives a low-cost, repeatable research step before AI drafting. It grounds topic selection in actual search impressions/queries rather than AI guesses.
- **Stage:** Keyword research, topic research, search intent, content prioritization, SEO performance analysis
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Mark includes caveats from others: GSC frontend can be unreliable for large sites, and regex filtering can inflate impression totals by aggregating page metrics.

### 3. Improve GEO visibility with visible-text improvements, not hidden hacks

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: If you're interested in 'GEO', there is a Princeton peer-reviewed paper from 2024…”
- **Date:** 28 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7465784114206523393-Z5S4](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7465784114206523393-Z5S4)
- **Recommendation:** For generative search visibility, focus on visible-page improvements such as quotes, statistics, citations, clearer wording, technical terms where appropriate, and readability. Avoid keyword stuffing, which the cited study found harmful.
- **Evidence:** “Every single method they tested is a change to the visible text of the page.” / “Quotes, stats, citations, clearer phrasing.” / “Keyword stuffing actively hurt visibility.”
- **Why it matters:** This source turns GEO into concrete content-quality actions. It supports an AI-era editing checklist: add evidence, cite sources, clarify writing, and make claims easier for AI systems and humans to parse.
- **Stage:** AI-search visibility, content quality, human editing, fact-checking, originality/evidence, on-page SEO
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** Mark is summarizing a study rather than presenting his own test. The final playbook should cite and verify the original Princeton/GEO paper before treating the uplift percentages as fixed benchmarks.

### 4. Validate, QA, and monitor SEO recommendations after the audit

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: Your SEO audit has no impact unless it is actually implemented correctly…”
- **Date:** 26 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7464975887546044417-RPhP](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7464975887546044417-RPhP)
- **Recommendation:** Do not stop at producing recommendations. Validate whether the issue matters to the business, discuss implementation difficulty with developers, QA fixes before they go live, and monitor the site afterward because changes break over time.
- **Evidence:** “Do we even care about this?” / “How difficult is this?” / “You should check the changes.” / “You need a system to flag this.”
- **Why it matters:** AI-powered content production can create a lot of recommendations quickly; Mark’s process prevents that from becoming shelfware or harmful implementation. This is especially useful for publishing governance and refresh workflows.
- **Stage:** SEO audit, prioritization, publishing QA, technical/content implementation, monitoring, content refreshing
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The post mentions Sitebulb and ContentKing, but Mark states he pays for both and was not paid for the post.

### 5. Use query fan-out to anticipate prompts, follow-up questions, and pages worth ranking

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “Casual AI search tool weekend free launch for those paying attention: QueryFan . com”
- **Date:** 30 May 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_casual-ai-search-tool-weekend-free-launch-activity-7466557456635207680-32dL](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_casual-ai-search-tool-weekend-free-launch-activity-7466557456635207680-32dL)
- **Recommendation:** Use query fan-out analysis to understand the prompts users may ask LLMs, the next likely questions in an AI conversation, and the related queries an LLM may fan out into. Use that intelligence to decide what supporting content or pages are worth ranking.
- **Evidence:** “Want to know the prompts specific groups are likely using on LLMs?” / “Predict the next most likely questions in the conversation?” / “Know the fan out queries the LLMs are doing.”
- **Why it matters:** This adds an AI-search research layer to traditional keyword research. It is useful for building briefs that serve both classic SERPs and conversational AI discovery paths.
- **Stage:** AI-search research, topic research, search intent, content briefs, AI-search visibility
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** The post is also a launch announcement for Mark’s own tool, so there is product bias. The playbook should describe the concept in tool-neutral terms where possible.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly third-party/company records, event posts, repeated AI-strategy warnings, technical tangents, tool launches with less direct SOP value, or sources better represented by selected intent, GSC, GEO, QA, and fan-out posts.

## Takeaway

Mark Williams-Cook’s overall viewpoint is that good AI-era SEO should be evidence-led, implementation-aware, and grounded in how search systems actually classify and retrieve information. He is skeptical of handing strategy to AI, but enthusiastic about using tools, models, APIs, and search data for specific analytical jobs. His strongest contribution to the playbook is intent research: using People Also Ask, GSC query patterns, query classification, and query fan-out to understand what users and AI systems are likely to need next. He also emphasizes that content quality for GEO comes from visible evidence and clarity, not hidden technical tricks or keyword stuffing.

## Strongest Recommendation

Use intent-proximity questions and real GSC query patterns to build or refresh content around the complete user journey, not just the primary keyword.

## Weakest / Least Supported

The GEO uplift percentages from the Princeton paper are useful but least supported inside the scraped material because Mark is summarizing another study. The final playbook should verify the original paper before using the exact numbers.
