## Selected Sources

### 1. Diagnose cannibalization before creating more content

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “📣 If your Search traffic has dropped after Google's March/April Core Algo update — STOP CREATING CONTENT FOR A SECOND.”
- **Date:** 10 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matt-kenyon-50959964_if-your-search-traffic-has-dropped-after-activity-7470505312207413248-rUdE](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matt-kenyon-50959964_if-your-search-traffic-has-dropped-after-activity-7470505312207413248-rUdE)
- **Recommendation:** Before producing more content after a traffic drop, diagnose whether existing pages are cannibalizing each other. Use Google Search Console to compare pre-drop and post-drop periods, identify losing queries, inspect which pages rank for those queries, then merge, realign, or prune pages based on intent and business value.
- **Evidence:** “STOP CREATING CONTENT FOR A SECOND.” / “Go into Google Search Console” / “You have three options from here: MERGE… DE-OPTIMIZE and RE-ALIGN… PRUNE & DELETE.”
- **Why it matters:** It prevents AI-powered content production from becoming content sprawl. This is a strong SOP checkpoint before briefing or publishing more pages.
- **Stage:** Content audit, content refreshing, keyword cannibalization, on-page SEO, publishing governance
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The post references Google’s March/April core update effect as “apparently” cracking down on similar user sentiment. The diagnostic workflow is strong, but the exact algorithm-cause claim should be treated as Matt’s interpretation unless verified with broader evidence.

### 2. Start from business first principles before choosing SEO/AI tactics

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “So many brands lose their way by getting caught in the weeds of SEO/marketing tactics while losing sight of first principles.”
- **Date:** 10 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matt-kenyon-50959964_so-many-brands-lose-their-way-by-getting-activity-7470444870248349696-uDTa](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matt-kenyon-50959964_so-many-brands-lose-their-way-by-getting-activity-7470444870248349696-uDTa)
- **Recommendation:** When overwhelmed by SEO and AI tactics, simplify back to the core offer and rebuild the strategy upward from “what do we sell?” Prune tactics that do not move the business forward.
- **Evidence:** “The key is simplifying things so much that you feel silly. All the way down to ‘what do we sell again?’ And then work your way upwards.”
- **Why it matters:** It gives the playbook a strategic filter before topic selection, prompting, drafting, or scaling. AI content production should begin with offer, audience, and business value rather than tool enthusiasm.
- **Stage:** Audience and business goals, content strategy, prioritization
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Caveat:** Strong strategic principle, but it is not a detailed production workflow by itself.

### 3. Build SEO content through a repeatable intent-to-publish workflow

- **Type:** YouTube
- **Video:** “How to Write SEO Content That Ranks in Google and AI Search”
- **Date:** Not available in scraped file
- **URL:** [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QL_fgTOS4pI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QL_fgTOS4pI)
- **Recommendation:** Turn one keyword into one content piece through a structured workflow: check search intent, analyze current ranking pages, create a competitor-informed outline, use AI for missing angles and fresh user signals, draft section by section, optimize for entities and AI-citable facts, add internal links, complete a pre-publish quality review, publish/export, then revisit performance at 30/60/90 days.
- **Evidence:** “If you skip it, everything that follows is wasted — no amount of optimization fixes a content type mismatch.” / “Your outline should be about 70% proven topics and 30% unique angles.” / “I’ll usually aim to add about 3-5 internal links within this new post.”
- **Why it matters:** This is Matt’s most complete SOP source for AI-powered SEO content production. It covers almost the whole pipeline from intent validation to refresh.
- **Stage:** Search intent, SERP analysis, content briefs, AI-assisted research, drafting, on-page SEO, internal linking, quality control, publishing, refreshing
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The workflow is demonstrated through Surfer’s Content Editor and Matt partnered with Surfer for the video. The general SOP is useful, but tool-specific claims such as scoring/citation likelihood should be separated from tool-agnostic best practice.

### 4. Create comparison, alternatives, and category-defining content for AI-search visibility

- **Type:** YouTube
- **Video:** “How to Get ChatGPT to Recommend Your Brand Over Competitors”
- **Date:** Not available in scraped file
- **URL:** [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsjS69LLKOk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsjS69LLKOk)
- **Recommendation:** Improve AI-search visibility by building content assets that repeatedly associate the brand with its category, competitors, and buyer-intent queries: comparison pages, competitor-alternative articles, buyer guides, category-defining content, niche positioning, and third-party review signals.
- **Evidence:** “Every brand has built five to ten or more dedicated ‘Brand X vs Competitor Y’ pages, plus ‘[Competitor] alternatives’ articles.” / “LLMs give more confident recommendations when a brand has a clear, specific position.”
- **Why it matters:** It turns AI-search visibility into concrete content types that can be briefed, produced, internally linked, and measured.
- **Stage:** Topic research, competitor/SERP analysis, bottom-funnel content, AI-search visibility, positioning, measurement
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Matt’s causal explanation about LLM recommendations is partly inferred from observed patterns. The video also promotes Surfer AI Tracker/Content Editor, so tool incentives should be noted.

### 5. Use AI for strategic options, but validate before shipping

- **Type:** YouTube
- **Video:** “ChatGPT, Claude & Gemini Built My SEO Strategy”
- **Date:** Not available in scraped file
- **URL:** [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APKFGK85-oE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APKFGK85-oE)
- **Recommendation:** Use AI to generate SEO strategy options, but do not treat AI output as final. Before turning AI recommendations into content plans or campaigns, manually inspect the website, check whether the model actually used the site, verify claims, challenge outdated tactics, separate AI-search surfaces, and replace weak ranking-only KPIs with business-relevant measures.
- **Evidence:** “AI does not know what’s true. It knows what’s been written.” / “Before I would ever trust any AI generated strategy, I want to see evidence that it inspected the live site.” / “They don’t just accept the KPI because the client wants it, they challenge it.”
- **Why it matters:** It gives the SOP a validation layer for AI-generated briefs, outlines, strategies, and KPIs. This is especially important when scaling content production with AI.
- **Stage:** AI prompting, strategy validation, fact-checking, website audit, SERP validation, AI-search measurement, KPI design
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The test used a fictional company, so the exact model comparison should not be generalized too heavily. The human-check workflow is still highly usable.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly brief LinkedIn principles, tool/model reactions, promotional teasers for selected YouTube sources, freelancing/business posts, and compiled reports used only for recovery/cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Matt Kenyon’s overall viewpoint is that AI can speed up SEO strategy and content production, but it should not replace search evidence, business judgment, or human quality control. He repeatedly pushes marketers back toward first principles: understand the offer, inspect the SERP, match intent, verify claims, and measure business-relevant outcomes. His strongest contribution to the playbook is a practical, step-by-step content workflow that combines AI-assisted research and drafting with human editing, internal linking, and post-publish review. He is also useful for AI-search visibility, especially comparison and alternatives content, but he treats AI-search tactics as context-dependent rather than universally valuable.

## Strongest Recommendation

Check search intent and SERP reality before writing, then build a structured outline that combines competitor-proven coverage with unique angles, AI-assisted research, and human examples.

## Weakest / Least Supported

The claim that comparison/listicle/category content directly improves ChatGPT recommendations is plausible and supported by observed examples, but the causal mechanism is partly inferred. It should be used as a working hypothesis and measured, not treated as settled law.

## Differences With Other Experts

- Matt appears more cautious than highly automation-forward experts: he supports AI-assisted workflows but emphasizes human validation before shipping.
- He may be more tool-workflow oriented because several strongest YouTube sources are Surfer-partnered; other experts may offer more tool-agnostic or custom-system approaches.
- His AI-search recommendation style is pragmatic and conditional: comparison/listicle tactics only matter if they strengthen the brand relative to competitors. This may conflict with experts who recommend broad AI-search visibility plays more aggressively.
