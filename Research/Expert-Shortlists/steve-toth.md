## Selected Sources

### 1. Turn long AI work sessions into reusable SOPs before context is lost

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “AI Tip: Before you run out of context window, make Claude write the SOP for the all the hard work you just put in.”
- **Date:** 10 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_ai-tip-before-you-run-out-of-context-window-activity-7470459834891395073-umZM](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_ai-tip-before-you-run-out-of-context-window-activity-7470459834891395073-umZM)
- **Recommendation:** At the end of a complex AI-assisted build or content workflow, ask the model to convert the session’s refinements, guidelines, and decisions into an SOP/master prompt before the context window runs out. Save that SOP as a reusable prompt, project instruction, Claude Skill, or process asset.
- **Evidence:** “Create an SOP with a master prompt…” / “The deck was the deliverable, but the SOP is the asset.” / “Ship the work, then ship the SOP.”
- **Why it matters:** This is directly applicable to scaling AI-powered SEO content production. It turns one-off prompting into reusable operating procedure, preserving editorial preferences, prompt refinements, quality rules, and production knowledge.
- **Stage:** AI prompting, SOP creation, workflow documentation, scaling content production, quality control
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The example is a pitch deck, not an SEO article, but the workflow-transfer principle clearly applies to content production.

### 2. Structure AI-citable answers with declarative sentences and low dependency hops

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “AI citable content and 'old school' featured snippet optimization share the same structural rule.”
- **Date:** 18 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_seonotebook-ainotebook-activity-7473358900809883648-yaP](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_seonotebook-ainotebook-activity-7473358900809883648-yaP)_
- **Recommendation:** Write answer sections as short, declarative, self-contained statements immediately after relevant headings or questions. Reduce dependency hops between subject, predicate, and supporting detail so humans, Google, and LLMs can extract the answer without extra context.
- **Evidence:** “Declarative sentences. Minimal dependencies. Subject + Predicate.” / “Three sentences. Stand alone.” / “Answer Capsules.”
- **Why it matters:** This gives a concrete writing and editing rule for AI-search visibility. It can become a content QA checklist item for FAQs, definitions, product claims, and comparison sections.
- **Stage:** AI-assisted drafting, human editing, on-page SEO, featured snippets, AI-search visibility, content quality
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Steve cites an SEL study of about 2 million ChatGPT sessions, but the scraped post does not include the study link or methodology. The writing rule is practical even if the exact citation statistic needs verification.

### 3. Use Google’s helpful-content questions as an AI-assisted content QA layer

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “I built a GPT/Claude Project that grades any page against Google's own 32 helpful-content questions.”
- **Date:** 12 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_i-built-a-gptclaude-project-that-grades-activity-7471184588908785665-26rR](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_i-built-a-gptclaude-project-that-grades-activity-7471184588908785665-26rR)
- **Recommendation:** Convert Google’s “Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content” self-assessment questions into a GPT/Claude Project that scores pages consistently, explains each score, identifies top fixes, and can help rewrite weak sections.
- **Evidence:** “Paste in a page and you get… PASS, FAIL, PARTIAL, or N/A.” / “The top 3 to 5 fixes.” / “An A is not a ranking guarantee.”
- **Why it matters:** This is one of the clearest AI-assisted editorial QA workflows in the sample. It turns Google’s broad guidance into a repeatable review process for drafts and refreshes.
- **Stage:** Human editing, fact-checking, content quality, Google helpful content, content refreshing, QA
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** The workflow is based on Google’s public questions, but Steve’s scoring rubric is his own. The final playbook should avoid implying that a high score guarantees rankings.

- **Confidence:** Medium

### 5. Create owned content for buyer deal-breaker questions, even when AI cites third-party sources

- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Opening:** “'95% of our citations come from third-party content. So why bother creating our own?'”
- **Date:** 2 June 2026
- **URL:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_seonotebook-ainotebook-activity-7467561003183050752-dh2B](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevetothjr_seonotebook-ainotebook-activity-7467561003183050752-dh2B)
- **Recommendation:** Do not conclude that owned content is useless just because broad AI prompt trackers show many third-party citations. Build owned pages around high-value buyer deal-breaker questions such as integrations, security, pricing, onboarding, and capabilities, because those answers often need to come from the brand itself.
- **Evidence:** “What an LLM cites depends entirely on what you ask it.” / “Functional queries flip toward owned pages.” / “Create content at the intersection of your buyer's deal-breaker decisions.”
- **Why it matters:** This is a strong content strategy principle for B2B AI search. It tells teams which content to produce: not generic “best tools” pages, but precise answer assets that prevent buyers from being misinformed or disqualified by stale third-party sources.
- **Stage:** Audience and business goals, search intent, content briefs, AI-search visibility, product-led content, publishing
- **Confidence:** High
- **Caveat:** Steve references an Omniscient Digital citation analysis, but the scraped post does not include the original report link. The strategic distinction between broad prompts and deal-breaker prompts is still useful.

## Rejected Sources

Excluded sources were mostly personal/promotional posts, newsletter teasers, overlapping AEO framing, specialized entity/acquisition advice, or useful ideas without the full workflow present in the scrape. The existing report was used only for cross-checking.

## Takeaway

Steve Toth’s overall viewpoint is that AI search is not just about being cited; it is about whether AI systems understand and recommend the brand accurately during real buyer decision moments. He treats AEO as a mix of SEO, brand representation, citation-surface analysis, and content quality. His practical content advice focuses on making claims easy to extract, verify, and trust, while using AI to create reusable workflows and QA systems. He is especially useful for B2B teams because he repeatedly connects AI visibility to deal-breaker questions such as integrations, security, pricing, onboarding, and product capabilities.

## Strongest Recommendation

Create owned, highly retrievable content that answers buyer deal-breaker questions, then structure those answers in short, declarative, evidence-backed answer capsules.

## Weakest / Least Supported

His specific claims about tracker costs, API economics, and citation-data coverage are useful but least supported inside the scraped material. They should be verified before becoming tool or budget recommendations in the final playbook.
