# Master Recommendations

**Project:** AI-Powered SEO Content Production Playbook

**Source constraint:** This database is derived exclusively from the approved expert shortlist files in Research/Expert-Shortlists/. Raw LinkedIn posts, YouTube transcripts, external sources, and any newly collected material were not used directly.

**Method note:** Each shortlist recommendation is listed separately, even when it overlaps with another expert. The approved shortlist files provide a single confidence assessment, which is reported here as both **Evidence Strength** and **Confidence Level** for consistency across the database.

## Business Goals

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Aimee Jurenka
- **Recommendation:** AI visibility requires alignment across the full brand ecosystem: what the brand and products represent, the topics the brand wants to be associated with, how informational content supports that narrative, and whether website and third-party signals tell the same story.
- **Why:** It adds a strategic pre-brief step: before producing AI-assisted content, teams should define the brand meaning and topic associations the content needs to reinforce.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “AI visibility isn’t just about content—it’s about alignment.” (22 May 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/advanced-web-ranking_aivisibility-seo-searchstrategy-activity-7463546808716316672-ldSX](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/advanced-web-ranking_aivisibility-seo-searchstrategy-activity-7463546808716316672-ldSX)
- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Caveat:** This is an attributed summary from Advanced Web Ranking’s post about Aimee’s interview, not a full transcript. Use it as Aimee-attributed strategic guidance, but avoid overclaiming exact wording beyond the excerpt.
- **Confidence Level:** High

### Rec 2

- **Expert:** Freddie Chatt
- **Recommendation:** Brands should stop publishing content just to publish. Every content asset should have a defined job: earn links, drive sales, build awareness, or grow the email list.
- **Why:** This is a strong production-governance rule. It helps decide what to brief, what to reject, and how to measure success after publishing.
- **Evidence Strength:** Medium
- **Source:** “Most content is forgotten the second it’s published.” (4 June 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_most-content-is-forgotten-the-second-its-activity-7468194950720024578-rddU](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_most-content-is-forgotten-the-second-its-activity-7468194950720024578-rddU)

- **Caveat:** The post is partly a resource announcement and does not provide examples or measurement criteria for each content job.
- **Confidence Level:** Medium

### Rec 3

- **Expert:** Matt Kenyon

## Audience Research

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Kai Cromwell
- **Recommendation:** For high-ticket ecommerce products, use Claude to collect buyer pain points from Reddit, Amazon reviews, YouTube comments, and low-star competitor reviews. Then write a buying guide covering costs, delivery, installation, maintenance, objections, and other pre-purchase concerns. Add internal links from supporting content to the guide and from the guide to money pages.
- **Why:** This is Kai’s clearest AI-powered content-production workflow. It combines audience research, AI-assisted synthesis, buyer education, internal linking, and commercial page support.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “Do this if you sell a $500+ product.” (12 June 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_do-this-if-you-sell-a-500-product-1-activity-7471056302073569280-c2v-](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_do-this-if-you-sell-a-500-product-1-activity-7471056302073569280-c2v-)

- **Confidence Level:** High

## Topic Selection

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Aimee Jurenka
- **Recommendation:** For small brands, start by clarifying core topics, turning them into indexable blog categories, supporting those categories with focused content, strengthening internal links, and using simple external discovery signals.
- **Why:** This is a practical content-architecture SOP for smaller sites that cannot produce large volumes of content. It emphasizes focused topic structure over brute-force publishing.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “For small brands, the challenge isn’t just ranking - it’s getting understood.” (26 May 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/advanced-web-ranking_aiseo-searchstrategy-digitalmarketing-activity-7464996368730951681-FKDm](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/advanced-web-ranking_aiseo-searchstrategy-digitalmarketing-activity-7464996368730951681-FKDm)

- **Confidence Level:** High

- **Confidence Level:** Medium

## Keyword Research

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Aleyda Solís
- **Recommendation:** In AI Mode/search environments, complement classic keyword research with prompt, task, constraint, scenario, and follow-up journey research. Analyze the journey rather than only the first query, and optimize beyond content alone into entity, product, local, feed, and availability readiness.
- **Why:** This expands the research stage of the playbook for AI search. It helps teams create content around real decision journeys, product attributes, local constraints, and task completion rather than isolated keywords.
- **Evidence Strength:** Medium
- **Source:** “Google just shared a new report on how people are using AI Mode in the US.” (24 May 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_google-just-shared-a-new-report-on-how-activity-7464243547047813120-8AJM](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_google-just-shared-a-new-report-on-how-activity-7464243547047813120-8AJM)

- **Confidence Level:** Medium

- **Recommendation:** Ecommerce brands should avoid starting with overly broad, highly competitive head terms. Instead, identify the product’s USP and target long-tail commercial queries that are easier to rank for and more likely to convert.
- **Why:** This is a clear keyword-research SOP rule for ecommerce content production. It helps teams brief product/category/supporting content around realistic, conversion-oriented opportunities rather than vanity keywords.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “The #1 mistake ecommerce sites make with SEO:” (29 May 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_the-1-mistake-ecommerce-sites-make-with-activity-7466016740032819200-1Txv](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/freddiechatt_the-1-mistake-ecommerce-sites-make-with-activity-7466016740032819200-1Txv)

- **Confidence Level:** High

### Rec 3

- **Expert:** Kai Cromwell
- **Recommendation:** Classify a product line in as many commercially meaningful ways as possible, then build and rank a page for each attribute/category variation rather than only targeting the broad head term.
- **Why:** This is a practical topic/keyword expansion method for ecommerce. It helps convert product attributes into content/page briefs for long-tail category demand.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “99% of SEO campaigns leave money on the table.” (5 June 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_99-of-seo-campaigns-leave-money-on-the-table-activity-7468519591481319424-mx5l](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kai-cromwell_99-of-seo-campaigns-leave-money-on-the-table-activity-7468519591481319424-mx5l)

- **Confidence Level:** High

### Rec 4

- **Expert:** Mark Williams-Cook
- **Recommendation:** Use regex filters in Google Search Console to segment real query data into informational, comparison, product/service, transactional, navigational, SaaS/tool, and long-question patterns. Use those query groups to uncover content opportunities and diagnose what your site is already ranking for.
- **Why:** This gives a low-cost, repeatable research step before AI drafting. It grounds topic selection in actual search impressions/queries rather than AI guesses.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: Instead of spicy 'probably ok' AI, you can reliably* filter your GSC query data…” (11 June 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7470766265666580480-FvFN](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7470766265666580480-FvFN)

- **Confidence Level:** High

## Search Intent

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Mark Williams-Cook
- **Recommendation:** Use “time to result” thinking and People Also Ask-style intent-proximity questions to identify what users are likely to ask next. Compare those questions against an existing page to find which related needs are fully answered, partially answered, or missing.
- **Why:** This is a highly practical way to turn audience intent into a content brief or refresh brief. It helps teams move beyond one keyword and build pages that solve the full search journey.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “Unsolicited #SEO tip: Google uses TTR (Time To Result) as an internal success metric…” (17 June 2026) — [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7472946532959457281-1Hbz](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markseo_seo-activity-7472946532959457281-1Hbz)
- **Type:** LinkedIn
- **Caveat:** The post references independent correlation studies but does not include the underlying data in the scraped material. The method is still operationally strong.
- **Confidence Level:** High

### Rec 2

- **Expert:** Nicolas Gorroño
- **Recommendation:** Structure content for longer, conversational, task-based AI search queries. Rewrite headings as complete questions, answer user tasks directly, add follow-up question blocks, include multimodal assets, optimize image metadata, and test whether pages are usable by AI agents.
- **Why:** It gives practical page-structure rules for AI-era content briefs and editing. It is especially useful for turning keyword-based content into question-led, task-led, AI search-friendly content.
- **Evidence Strength:** Medium
- **Source:** “Google AI Mode and AI Search Content Strategy” (Not available in scraped file) — [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbEAH1NzB5c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbEAH1NzB5c)
- **Type:** YouTube
- **Caveat:** The `llms.txt` and agentic-readiness claims need caution. Google guidance does not support treating `llms.txt` as required for Google AI visibility, so that part should be marked experimental.
- **Confidence Level:** Medium

## Content Brief

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Matt Kenyon
- **Recommendation:** Turn one keyword into one content piece through a structured workflow: validate intent, analyze ranking pages, build a competitor-informed outline, use AI for missing angles and user signals, draft section by section, optimize for entities and AI-citable facts, add internal links, run pre-publish QA, publish/export, then review performance at 30/60/90 days.
- **Why:** This is Matt’s most complete SOP source for AI-powered SEO content production, covering intent validation through refresh.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “How to Write SEO Content That Ranks in Google and AI Search” (Not available in scraped file) — [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QL_fgTOS4pI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QL_fgTOS4pI)
- **Type:** YouTube
- **Caveat:** The workflow is demonstrated through Surfer’s Content Editor and Matt partnered with Surfer for the video. The general SOP is useful, but tool-specific claims such as scoring/citation likelihood should be separated from tool-agnostic best practice.
- **Confidence Level:** High

### Rec 2

- **Expert:** Nathan Gotch
- **Recommendation:** Treat SEO content production as an end-to-end system: choose a realistic keyword, build a brand knowledge base, research Google and AI-platform competitors, create an SEO outline, draft with AI, fact-check, design for conversions, publish, track performance, add internal links, expand topical support, and build authority.
- **Why:** This is Nathan’s strongest complete SOP source, mapping directly to research, briefing, drafting, QA, publishing, tracking, linking, topical expansion, and authority building.
- **Evidence Strength:** High
- **Source:** “How To Rank SEO Content In Google And AI Platforms” (Not available in scraped transcript) — [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AaSyn9YSNYQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AaSyn9YSNYQ)

### Rec 3

- **Why:** This is a strong content strategy principle for B2B AI search. It tells teams which content to produce: not generic “best tools” pages, but precise answer assets that prevent buyers from being misinformed or disqualified by stale third-party sources.

- **Source:** “6 AI SEO Skills That Will Matter Most in 2026” (Not available in scraped transcript) — [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mbCfRlY7elM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mbCfRlY7elM)

## Publishing

### Rec 1

- **Expert:** Aleyda Solís
- **Recommendation:** Do not assume AI assistants render JavaScript when grounding answers. If important content is injected client-side, lazy-loaded, hidden behind hydrated tabs, or absent from raw HTML, it may effectively be invisible to major AI assistants. Use server logs rather than chatbot self-reports to verify what AI systems actually fetched.
