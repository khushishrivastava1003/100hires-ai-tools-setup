## Executive Summary
In this video, Matt Kenyon studies five smaller B2B brands that appear in ChatGPT recommendations ahead of much larger competitors. He analyzed 35 buying-intent queries across 15 software categories using Surfer's AI Tracker. The brands he highlights include TalentLMS, Chili Piper, Help Scout, Vanta, and Trainual.

The key pattern is that these companies are not winning AI visibility by accident. They use content strategies that repeatedly associate their brands with important competitors, categories, and buyer questions. Matt identifies several moves: comparison page ecosystems, competitor alternatives pages, category-defining content, category reframing, niche positioning, and strong third-party review signals. The main lesson is that AI search visibility can be influenced by creating the right content assets across the web.

## Key Insights

### Insight 1: Comparison pages influence AI recommendations
Brands that publish "Brand vs Competitor" pages create repeated associations between themselves and category leaders.

### Insight 2: Competitor alternatives pages are powerful
Writing about alternatives to competitors helps smaller brands appear when buyers research competing products.

### Insight 3: Category-defining content builds authority
Brands like Vanta win by educating the market before buyers are ready to purchase.

### Insight 4: Category reframing creates a new recommendation slot
Chili Piper and Trainual avoid direct competition by defining themselves in a more specific category.

### Insight 5: Reviews act as AI trust signals
G2 and Capterra ratings help AI systems justify recommending smaller brands over larger competitors.

* Build comparison pages for major competitors.
* Create alternatives articles targeting competitor searches.
* Publish buyer guides that define the category.
* Develop a clear niche position rather than claiming to serve everyone.

* Track whether ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity mention your brand for buyer-intent queries.
